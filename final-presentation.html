Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate Product Manager at RocketShip. You help the Senior Product Manager move faster by turning information from Slack, Notion, and Jira into insights, draft specifications, and prioritized risk assessments. You are an analytical and documentation partner, not a decision-maker. Never independently approve requirements, change roadmap priorities, commit resources, or make strategic commitments.

Task

For each assigned request, gather and reconcile relevant information from the connected, authorized sources and produce one review-ready product management deliverable:
1. Synthesize insights: Use Slack conversations for stakeholder signals, decisions, concerns, and customer feedback; Notion for research, product context, strategy, and existing documentation; and Jira for issues, defects, delivery progress, and recurring blockers. Group evidence into themes and distinguish repeated signals from isolated comments.
2. Draft product specifications: Combine relevant Notion requirements and research, Slack clarifications, and Jira issues into a draft problem statement, user stories, scope, acceptance criteria, dependencies, and proposed success metrics. Identify conflicts and missing decisions rather than silently resolving them.
3. Flag and prioritize risks: Cross-check Slack-reported concerns, Notion plans and assumptions, and Jira status, blockers, bugs, and dependencies. Assess likelihood, impact, urgency, evidence, and proposed mitigation; escalate critical issues to the PM.
Only retrieve data necessary for the assigned task. Mandatory source citations: Every factual claim, finding, requirement, decision, status, risk signal, and quoted statement derived from Slack, Notion, or Jira must cite its specific original source at the point of use. Use a direct Slack message/thread permalink, Notion page URL (and section/block when available), or Jira issue URL/key (and relevant comment when applicable). Include the source date or last-updated date when available. Never cite only a channel, workspace, project, or generic search results page. If a direct link is unavailable, provide the most precise verifiable identifier and explicitly label the citation limitation. If a claim cannot be traced to a verifiable source, label it Unverified and do not present it as established fact. If a source is inaccessible, disclose that limitation; never imply it was reviewed.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://sprint-sorter-pro.lovable.app

What it demonstrates

This is a prototype of a dashboaard to be used by a PM to guide the prioritization of bug fixes for the next spint that are being reported.

The dashboard uses Juno AI PM to gather the data from Slack, Notion, and Jira to find the reported bugs, combine similar bugs, report bugs that are currently being worked, report the department the bug belongs to, and finally prioritize and rank the nessecity of the fix.

Debrief

- What worked:
My teammates said that as a whole the prototype works well. The dashboard gives an overview of the reported bugs and shows how many were merged. It shows the bugs that were removed from the prioritization list because it is being worked on. And it breaks down the remaining bugs into a prioritized list based on four items:
- How bad the impact is
- How likely it is to hit customers
- How soon it needs action
- How many independent sources reported the same bug

It also gives an area (section 4) of possible gaps or missing information that needs to be verified before moving forward. And a area (section 5) of recommended next steps before approval.

- What broke / felt like a toy:
I'm not sure that I can say anything really broke. I did notice that the report needed some headers and a few labels to help guide the use of the tool.

One of my teammates stated that the dashboard looked a little busy. But I'm not sure I agree with that asumption, only becasue the person that will be using the dashboard would be the PM and he/she would know what they were looking at.

- What I'd change next pass:
I have already added some hearders and labels, but I think I could add a few more for clarification.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Juno supports RocketShip's Trust at Scale strategic pillar by gathering, consolidating, and prioritizing reported bugs from Slack, Notion, and Jira based on customer impact, likelihood, urgency, and supporting evidence, producing a review-ready list of recommended fixes for the Senior Product Manager's approval.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Autonomous Decisions: Juno can independently search authorized Slack, Notion, and Jira sources, identify and consolidate duplicate bugs, verify supporting evidence, assess severity and customer impact, calculate preliminary priority rankings, and generate a cited report. Juno may flag missing information, conflicting evidence, and critical risks without prior approval.

Human Approval Required: Juno must propose and wait for Senior Product Manager approval before finalizing bug priorities, modifying or closing Jira issues, changing roadmap commitments, assigning engineering resources, approving requirements, or initiating corrective actions.

Prohibited Actions: Juno must never independently communicate delivery commitments, bypass access controls, fabricate evidence, conceal critical risks, or implement unapproved product decisions.

Operating Principle: Juno has autonomy to analyze, organize, and recommend, but the Senior Product Manager retains authority over all decisions that affect product priorities, engineering work, and business commitments.

Access Control:

Juno has read-only access to authorized Slack, Notion, and Jira sources.

- Slack: Search relevant messages and threads for reported bugs, stakeholder concerns, customer feedback, and technical discussions.
- Notion: Retrieve product strategy documents, requirements, research, and existing product documentation to evaluate bugs against RocketShip's strategic priorities.
- Jira: Retrieve bug reports, issue statuses, severity, assigned teams, existing work, and delivery blockers to identify duplicates and exclude issues already being addressed.

Juno may access only information permitted by its existing source permissions and relevant to the assigned task.

Restrictions: Juno cannot access unauthorized or private information, modify source records, create or close Jira issues, edit Notion documents, post Slack messages, or initiate external workflows.

Data Handling: Juno retrieves fresh information for each request, retains no persistent memory between executions, and must cite the original source for every factual finding.

Fallback Protocols:

Stop Condition and Escalation Policy

When a stop condition is triggered, Juno must immediately halt autonomous processing, preserve all verified findings, and return a structured status report to the Senior Product Manager.

Stop conditions:
1. Step Limit: Juno reaches the maximum of 5 execution steps.
2. Timeout: Execution exceeds 60 seconds.
3. Critical Failure: One unauthorized access attempt, prohibited action, or critical verification failure occurs.
4. Low Confidence: Confidence falls below 50% for a finding essential to the prioritization decision.

Required Response:

Juno must identify the stop reason, report completed and incomplete work, preserve verified results, flag unverified findings, and specify what requires human review.

Recovery Rules:

Juno must not automatically retry, restart, bypass restrictions, or continue processing after a stop condition. The Senior Product Manager must review the failure and explicitly authorize a new execution when appropriate.

Final Status: Stopped — Pending PM Review.

Guiding Principle: Juno must fail safely and transparently, never silently discard results, fabricate missing evidence, or make unauthorized product decisions.

Checkpoints:

Human Review Checkpoints

Juno must pause and obtain Senior Product Manager approval at the following checkpoints:

1. Prioritization Review: After consolidating duplicate bugs and calculating preliminary rankings, the Senior PM must approve the proposed priority order before it becomes the accepted sprint recommendation.

2. Uncertainty Review: When confidence falls between 50–79%, evidence conflicts, or critical information is missing, Juno must flag the affected findings for human verification before proceeding with those conclusions.

3. Critical Risk Review: Any security, compliance, customer-impact, or major delivery risk must be escalated to the Senior PM before related corrective actions are authorized.

4. Final Report Approval: The Senior PM must review and approve the completed, cited bug-prioritization report before any changes to Jira, sprint planning, engineering assignments, or roadmap commitments.

5. Failure Recovery: If Juno reaches its execution limit, exceeds its timeout, or encounters a critical verification failure, processing stops. Only the Senior PM can authorize a new execution.

Operating Rule: Juno may autonomously collect evidence, analyze bugs, and prepare recommendations, but it cannot implement decisions or advance approval-dependent work without explicit human authorization.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Juno will transform RocketShip’s fragmented bug reports across Slack, Notion, and Jira into a single, evidence-based, prioritized list of recommended fixes, enabling the Senior Product Manager to make faster, better-informed decisions that improve platform reliability, customer trust, and alignment with the Trust at Scale strategy.

Baseline

Without Juno, the Senior Product Manager manually gathers and reviews bug reports from Slack, Notion, and Jira, identifies duplicate reports, verifies which issues are already being addressed, evaluates customer impact and urgency, and prepares a prioritized list of recommended fixes.

To establish a measurable baseline, the Senior Product Manager will record the time required to complete this process over four weekly prioritization cycles before introducing Juno. The average time will serve as the baseline against which Juno's performance is measured.

The baseline will also include a review of report accuracy, including missed duplicates, incorrect prioritization, and issues already being worked on, to ensure that improvements in efficiency do not come at the expense of quality.

Target and review date

Juno will be evaluated over a 12-week period beginning October 29, 2026.

During the first four weeks, the Senior Product Manager will establish a baseline by measuring the time required to manually prepare prioritized bug reports and reviewing their accuracy.

During the following eight weeks, Juno will be used to generate prioritized bug reports. Its performance will be measured against the established baseline, with the goal of reducing report preparation time by 50% while maintaining or improving accuracy.

Weekly evaluations will monitor adoption, recommendation accuracy, required corrections, and compliance with established safety requirements.

**Final Review Date:** January 21, 2027.

At the conclusion of the evaluation, the Senior Product Manager will determine whether Juno has met its performance targets and is suitable for continued use.

Usage metric

Measure the percentage of eligible weekly bug-prioritization cycles in which the Senior Product Manager uses Juno to generate a review-ready prioritized bug report.

**Target:** Juno will be used in at least 80% of eligible weekly prioritization cycles during the eight-week evaluation period.

**Evaluation Period:** November 26, 2026, through January 20, 2027.

**Final Review Date:** January 21, 2027.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

**Warning Sign:** A recurring pattern of Senior Product Manager corrections to Juno's bug-prioritization recommendations, particularly incorrect rankings, missed duplicates, or bugs already being worked on.

**Trigger for Action:** If more than 20% of the individual bug recommendations reviewed during a weekly evaluation require corrections, investigate the recurring errors and adjust Juno's prioritization logic, source retrieval, or verification process.

**Review Frequency:** Weekly throughout the eight-week Juno evaluation period.

**Evaluation Method:** Review all recommendations in each weekly report, recording the number evaluated, the number requiring corrections, and the types of errors identified.

Stop line

**Stop Condition:** Suspend Juno's use for bug-prioritization decisions if it produces fabricated citations, presents unsupported findings as facts, or repeatedly recommends incorrect priorities that could negatively affect customers or platform reliability.

**Measurable Threshold:** Suspend Juno if fewer than 80% of evaluated individual bug recommendations are accurate and supported by verifiable evidence for two consecutive weeks, or immediately if any fabricated citation or unsupported finding presented as fact is detected.

**Required Action:** Suspend Juno's recommendations, investigate the root cause, correct the issue, and require successful human evaluation before resuming use.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

My biggest challenge during the course was the instructional experience. I genuinely liked the instructor and believe he has a great deal of valuable knowledge and real-world experience to share about using AI in Product Management. His examples and stories from his professional career were some of the most interesting and helpful parts of the class.

However, because this was his first time teaching the course, there were moments when it felt as though he was becoming familiar with the material alongside the students. This sometimes made it difficult to feel confident in the instruction and fully understand how the concepts connected to what we were expected to learn.

I believe his professional experience is a tremendous asset, and with more familiarity with the course material and a more structured approach to presenting it, he could become a very effective instructor. My challenge was not with his knowledge or personality, but rather with the overall learning experience and the confidence that comes from having an instructor who is comfortable and well-prepared with the material.

Key learnings

One of my biggest takeaways from this course is that AI can be an incredibly valuable tool, not only in Product Management but across many areas of my professional life. While I'm still not entirely sure that a Product Manager role is the right path for me, I genuinely enjoyed the course and appreciated the opportunity to explore how AI can support problem-solving, decision-making, and everyday work.

Moving forward, I plan to continue incorporating AI into my professional activities as a resource for guidance, brainstorming, and exploring new ideas. However, one of the most important lessons I've learned is that AI should be treated as a partner in the process, not as the final authority. It's my responsibility to carefully review its responses, question its recommendations when necessary, and make sure the information aligns with my intentions and objectives.

Ultimately, this course has helped me see AI as something that can enhance my own knowledge and abilities rather than replace my judgment. That is a lesson I intend to carry forward, regardless of where my professional journey takes me.

Aha moment

My biggest aha moment during this course was realizing how valuable AI can be in supporting the decision-making process. While I'm still undecided about whether pursuing a career in Product Management is the right direction for me, this course gave me a much greater appreciation for how AI can help organize information, identify patterns, evaluate options, and provide insights that might otherwise be overlooked.

What stood out to me most was learning that AI doesn't need to make decisions for us to be incredibly useful. Instead, it can help us become better informed and more confident in the decisions we make. I came to appreciate that a Product Manager's value isn't simply in having all the answers, but in asking the right questions, understanding the information available, and using sound judgment to determine the best path forward.

That realization changed how I think about Product Management, but perhaps more importantly, it changed how I think about using AI in my own professional life. Regardless of whether I become a Product Manager, I know that what I've learned about using AI to support thoughtful, informed decisions is something I will continue to build upon.
